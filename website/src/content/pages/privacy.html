Privacy Policy

TidyByte’s iPhone cleanup tools process your local photo library on-device. The app has no TidyByte account or developer-operated server. In builds with the optional Google Photos source, the app connects directly to Google after you sign in, check storage quota, or select items. Google sends previews to your device for local scanning; photos are not sent to TidyByte.

Effective date: January 1, 2026 · Last updated: November 5, 2026

1. Overview

TidyByte’s iPhone photo-library tools use Apple’s Photos, Vision, and AVFoundation frameworks on your device. TidyByte has no account system or developer-operated server. The optional Google Photos source, when enabled in your build, communicates directly with Google and is described below.

Learn more about all cleanup features or download TidyByte.

2. Data we collect

TidyByte does not collect app-usage analytics or advertising data and does not operate a server that receives photos. Local iPhone photo cleanup does not send your library to TidyByte. In builds with Google Photos enabled, the app sends requests directly to Google for sign-in, storage quota, and only the media items you pick. Google returns selected previews to your device for on-device scanning. The App Store privacy label currently reads “Data Not Collected”; this policy also explains the optional requests made directly to Google.

3. How the app works

All processing happens on-device using Apple’s system frameworks:

  • Photos framework — reads from and writes to your local photo library.
  • Vision framework — generates feature prints for near-duplicate detection and analyzes image quality (blur, exposure).
  • AVFoundation — compresses videos and re-encodes photos on-device.
  • SwiftData — stores local app records (swipe decisions, compression history) in a sandboxed database on your phone.

TidyByte ships no third-party SDKs. Every line that touches your local library is auditable in the public source repository.

4. Permissions the app requests

Photo Library (read & write)

Required for the iPhone cleanup tools. TidyByte uses Apple’s Photos framework to read your local library and write compressed videos or converted Live Photos back to it. Those local-library photos are not sent to TidyByte.

Google Photos (optional)

This source is available only in builds configured for Google Photos. If you choose to connect it, Google handles sign-in and the app calls Google directly to read your storage quota and the media items you select in Google Photos Picker. TidyByte requests file-size information and downloads small previews from Google to your device for local scanning; it does not upload photos to TidyByte or delete items from your Google Photos account. Google processes the account and API requests under its own privacy policy. OAuth tokens are stored in this device’s Keychain. Signing out asks Google to revoke the token and removes it from the device.

Notifications

Optional. Requested only if you enable “Cleanup Reminders” in Settings. Used solely to schedule local reminders via the iOS notification system.

5. Data storage on your device

TidyByte stores a small amount of local data using SwiftData to power features and history:

  • SwipeRecord — asset local identifiers and your keep/delete decisions. The actual photo data is never copied.
  • CompressionRecord — bookkeeping for video and photo compression jobs.
  • StorageSnapshot — cached storage breakdown used by the dashboard.

This data never leaves your device and can be cleared at any time via Settings → Reset Swipe History inside the app.

6. Children’s privacy

TidyByte is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect app-usage or advertising data. If a build with Google Photos enabled is connected to a Google account, the direct Google requests described above apply.
